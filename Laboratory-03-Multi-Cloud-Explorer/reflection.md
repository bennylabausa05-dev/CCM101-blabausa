# Mission Reflection

Out of the three platforms, **Google Cloud Platform** impressed me the most. I already knew AWS was popular and Azure was tied closely to Microsoft, but I didn't realize how much of the modern AI/ML tooling world (like Kubernetes and TensorFlow) actually started at Google. Seeing that GCP is built on the same infrastructure that runs Google Search and YouTube made its performance claims feel more concrete rather than just marketing.

Even though AWS, Azure, and GCP compete with each other, they share a lot of the same basic building blocks. All three organize their infrastructure into **Regions** and smaller zones (Availability Zones or Zones) to spread out risk and improve availability. All three also offer a virtual machine service, an object storage service, a virtual networking layer, and an identity/access management system — they just use different names for them. This made the comparison easier than I expected, since once I understood one platform's structure, I could recognize the same pattern in the other two.

This activity also showed me how much **business requirements** drive the choice of platform, more than personal preference or hype. A startup cares about low upfront cost and easy scaling, a university cares about compatibility with what it already owns, and a research team cares about raw computing power for AI. None of these clients needed "the best cloud provider overall" — they needed the provider that solved their specific problem, which matches the guiding principle from the mission brief.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me to slow down and ask about the client's existing systems and goals before recommending a platform, instead of jumping straight to my personal favorite. It's a more analytical way of thinking than I expected from a "beginner" cloud lab.

Finally, my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio has grown a lot more organized after this mission. I now have a clear folder per lab, consistent Markdown documentation, and a habit of backing up my research with screenshots and reference links, which makes the whole repository look more like real project documentation than scattered notes.
